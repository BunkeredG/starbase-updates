STARSHIP FLIGHT 14 

Alias: Starlink Group 31-1
Booster 21 and Ship 41 will fly on Flight 14.

Flight 14 will be Starship's first orbital flight and will deploy the first operational Starlink V3 satellites into the Starlink constellation.
The booster, after its ascent burn, will aim to complete both a boostback and landing burn before splashing down in the Gulf of America. The ship, on the other hand, will attempt to reach orbit, a first for Starship. Once it completes its Orital Insertion Burn, the ship will orbit Earth 6 times, during which it will deploy the first 26 Starlink V3 satellites (3 of which have cameras to photograph Ship 41) to join the constellation, orbiting in Group 31 (also a first for Starlink). This mission payload carries 10 times the internet capacity of a standard Falcon 9 launch, proving Starship's viability to grow Starlink. After its 10 hour coast in orbit, Ship 41 will then complete a deorbit burn, re-enter Earth's atmosphere, and perform a landing burn, splashing down off the coast of Chile. If the ship has any problems during its flight, it can splashdown early either near Hawaii or in the Indian Ocean.

Previous Starship V3 Flights

FLIGHT 13 
Booster 20 and Ship 40 flew on Flight 13 on July 24th, 2026.

Flight 13 was the second test of Starship V3. Booster 20 completed most of its boostback burn, shutting down near the end due to potential ice clogging, and then failed to relight 5 of the planned 13 engines for its landing burn, resulting in a hard splashdown. Ship 40 successfully deployed real Starlink V3 satellites (though, since the flight path was suborbital, they did not join the constellation) for the first time, relit an engine in space, and splashed down in the Indian Ocean soft enough to remain in one piece.
FLIGHT 12 
Booster 19 and Ship 39 flew on Flight 12 on May 22nd, 2026.

Flight 12 was the first test flight of Starship V3. One Raptor 3 engine flamed out on ascent and, after stage separation, Booster 19 was unable to perform its boostback burn. One of Ship 39's RVAC engines flamed out on ascent, but despite that the rest of the flight was successful, deploying dummy Starlink V3 satellites before splashing down in the Indian Ocean.
