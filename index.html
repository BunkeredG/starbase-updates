STARSHIP FLIGHT 15 

Booster 22 and Ship 42 will fly on Flight 15.

Flight 15 will be Starship's first catch attempt of both the Booster and the Ship.
Booster 22, following its ascent burn, will aim to complete both a boostback and landing burn before attempting a catch at Pad 2, the first for a Super-Heavy V3 (though three Super-Heavy V1’s have been caught previously).

The ship, like last flight, will reach orbit with a short Orbital Insertion Burn ~26 minutes into flight. Once there, it will first deploy 26 more Starlink V3 satellites (3 of which have cameras to gather data on Ship 42) into Group 31, the same as Flight 14, before orbiting the Earth 14 times over the course of 22 hours to bring Ship 42’s path back over Starbase.

If something goes wrong and the mission must end early, SpaceX may elect to perform a contingency splashdown in the Indian Ocean (remaining suborbital), near Hawaii (after 1 hour and 30 minutes), off the coast of Chile (after 7 hours), and near Hawaii again (after 21 hours and 40 minutes).

However, if all remains nominal, Ship 42 will deorbit after 22 hours in space and attempt the first ever catch of a Ship. In the final minute, Ship 42 will descend from the north over South Padre Island, going briefly over the Gulf before swinging back towards the pad, flipping vertical, and performing a catch.

Previous Flights

FLIGHT 14 
Booster 21 and Ship 41 flew on Flight 14 on September 28th, 2026.

Flight 14 was the first orbital flight of Starship. Booster 21 lost one engine during the ascent burn and another during an otherwise successful boostback burn. It then executed a successful landing burn and simulated being caught by the chopsticks, a first for Starship V3. Ship 41, even though it lost one of its RVAC engines, successfully reached orbit and deployed the first operational Starlink V3 satellites. Ship 41 only completed 2 of its planned 6 orbits due to a lack of confidence in the stability of the remaining engines. It completed its deobrit burn and splashed down off the coast of Hawaii, a planned contingency location.
FLIGHT 13 
Booster 20 and Ship 40 flew on Flight 13 on July 24th, 2026.

Flight 13 was the second test of Starship V3. Booster 20 completed most of its boostback burn, shutting down near the end due to potential ice clogging, and then failed to relight 5 of the planned 13 engines for its landing burn, resulting in a hard splashdown. Ship 40 successfully deployed real Starlink V3 satellites (though, since the flight path was suborbital, they did not join the constellation) for the first time, relit an engine in space, and splashed down in the Indian Ocean soft enough to remain in one piece.
FLIGHT 12 
Booster 19 and Ship 39 flew on Flight 12 on May 22nd, 2026.

Flight 12 was the first test flight of Starship V3. One Raptor 3 engine flamed out on ascent and, after stage separation, Booster 19 was unable to perform its boostback burn. One of Ship 39's RVAC engines flamed out on ascent, but despite that the rest of the flight was successful, deploying dummy Starlink V3 satellites before splashing down in the Indian Ocean.
